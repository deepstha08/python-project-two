How ValueAtlas works

My stocks: undervalued, neutral or overvalued

Each of your stocks gets a 0–100 value score. Its P/E, forward P/E and PEG are compared with companies in the same region and sector from today's market scan (100 = cheapest), and the PEG is also judged on its own (below 1 = cheap for its growth, above 2 = expensive). The score is 65% peer comparison and 35% PEG. 60 or more = Undervalued, 40 or less = Overvalued, anything in between is Neutral. Until the first market scan finishes, simple rules of thumb are used instead. ETFs, commodity ETCs and loss-making companies are shown as "Not rated", because P/E-based valuation does not apply to them.

Trade desk: bullish or bearish on 9 timeframes

Type any ticker, or start typing a company name, to switch stocks. The app loads candles for 1m, 2m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 3h, 4h and 1D. The 3h and 4h candles are built from 1h candles, starting at the session open as TradingView does. It then runs more than 60 signals on each timeframe:

  • Trend (30%): all 15 moving averages from TradingView's Technical Ratings (SMA/EMA 10–200, Hull, VWMA, Ichimoku), plus Supertrend, Parabolic SAR, EMA 9/21, DMI/ADX, Aroon, linear regression, Heikin-Ashi and market structure (higher highs and higher lows).
  • Momentum (25%): TradingView's 11 oscillators with their own buy/sell rules (RSI, Stochastic, CCI, ADX, Awesome, Momentum, MACD, Stoch RSI, Williams %R, Bull Bear Power, Ultimate), plus RSI bias, MACD histogram, ROC, TRIX and RSI divergence.
  • Volume (12%): OBV, Chaikin Money Flow, MFI, VWAP, volume spikes, Force Index, Accumulation/Distribution.
  • Volatility (8%): Bollinger %B and squeeze breakouts, Keltner, Donchian 20 breakout, the previous session's pivot.
  • Patterns (10%): 17 candlestick patterns, 50-bar range breakouts, double tops and bottoms.
  • Strategies (15%): the current position of 10 classic strategies (EMA cross, MACD cross, Supertrend, SAR, Turtle 20/10, RSI-2, Bollinger reversion, golden cross, VWAP trend, 30-minute opening-range breakout), each backtested on the loaded candles.

Category averages are weighted into a score from −1 to +1. Above +0.4 is Strong Bullish and above +0.1 is Bullish; the same thresholds below zero give Bearish and Strong Bearish. Everything else is Neutral.

Reading the verdict honestly: "5m Bullish" means the 5-minute chart currently looks bullish. It is not a forecast that the price will be higher in 5 minutes. That's why every timeframe shows a hit rate: how often the score's direction matched the next candle on that stock's recent history. Short timeframes are usually close to 50% (a coin flip). Use the verdicts together, for example several timeframes agreeing, and manage your risk.

Data: candles come from Yahoo Finance. They're close to real-time for US stocks and usually 15–20 minutes delayed on other exchanges. The chart refreshes every 5 seconds on 1m and less often on longer timeframes. The TradingView live chart tab and the TradingView rating gauge are TradingView's own official widgets, showing their real-time data and ratings directly. ValueAtlas doesn't read data from them, because TradingView's terms forbid that.

Options (US stocks): max pain, call wall (resistance), put wall (support), put/call ratios by volume and open interest, at-the-money implied volatility, the expected move from the straddle, and unusual activity (volume above open interest). Levels can be drawn on the chart.

Only stocks tradable on Scalable Capital

With Only on Scalable Capital switched on (the default), the top 25 lists contain only stocks quoted on gettex, the Munich exchange Scalable Capital routes its orders to. Each day ValueAtlas downloads gettex's free, regulator-required instrument list and matches it against each stock's ISIN, which is looked up once and cached. A few stocks that Scalable offers only on Xetra or EIX may be missed. If the gettex list can't be downloaded (for example on a weekend before your first update), the previous list is used; with no list at all, availability is shown as not checked.

1 · Finding undervalued stocks

Each day the app scans the largest listed companies in each region with the Yahoo Finance screener: the US; the UK, Germany, France, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Italy, Spain, the Nordics, Belgium, Austria, Ireland and Portugal; and Japan, Hong Kong, mainland China, Korea, Taiwan, India and Singapore. The cheapest candidates on P/E and forward P/E (120 per region by default) are then looked up in detail.

A stock is ranked only if it has a positive P/E, forward P/E and PEG. Each ratio becomes a 0–100 cheapness percentile against its peers (lower ratio = higher score), and the Value score is the weighted average. With Compare within sector on, a stock is compared with at least five companies in the same region and sector, so a cheap bank is judged against banks, not software companies.

Value-trap flags warn when a low ratio might be deserved: falling earnings, high debt, negative free cash flow, unprofitable, or a forward P/E well above the trailing P/E, which means analysts expect earnings to drop.

2 · Bullish or bearish?

The outlook combines independent signals, each scored from −1 (bearish) to +1 (bullish):

  • Technical rating (30%): TradingView's published Technical Ratings formula (15 moving averages + 11 oscillators), calculated from Yahoo daily prices. It can differ slightly from the TradingView website.
  • Long-term trend (10%): price vs the 200-day average, and the 50-day vs the 200-day average.
  • Analyst consensus (25%) and price-target upside (15%): from Yahoo Finance.
  • Analyst revisions (8%): whether ratings improved or worsened over three months.
  • Options positioning (7%): the put/call open-interest ratio.
  • Finnhub analysts (5%): optional, needs a free key.

Missing signals are left out and the remaining weights are rescaled. Above +0.12 is Bullish and above +0.45 is Strong Bullish, with the same thresholds below zero for Bearish.

3 · Max pain

For every listed strike, the app adds up what all call and put holders would be paid if the stock settled there (intrinsic value × open interest × 100). Max pain is the strike where that total is lowest. This is the method OptionCharts documents. The open interest comes from Yahoo's option chains, so values can differ a little from optioncharts.io. Each stock has a button that opens its OptionCharts page for comparison. Values are saved at every refresh, so you can look up earlier dates on the Max pain board. Most European and Asian stocks have no options on Yahoo.

Why some sites are links only

OptionCharts', TradingView's and Investing.com's terms of service forbid automated downloading of their data, so ValueAtlas does not scrape them. It uses their published methods where available and links each stock to their pages. In a stock's details you can save what those sites show in My notes from other sites.

Important

Yahoo data is accessed via the open-source yfinance library. It is unofficial, can be delayed, and occasionally breaks until the library is updated. Run Update ValueAtlas from the Start menu to upgrade it. Low ratios can reflect real problems. This is a research tool, not investment advice.
